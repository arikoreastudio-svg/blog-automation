# Itaewon & Hannam-dong: Seoul's Most International Neighborhood

Most of Seoul's popular districts are distinctly Korean in flavor. Itaewon is the exception, for decades it's been the neighborhood where Seoul's international community, foreign residents, embassy staff, and visitors from every part of the world have gathered, and that mix shows up in the streets, the restaurants, and the general feel of the place.

This guide covers what Itaewon actually is, its international food scene and the Muslim-friendly area around Seoul's central mosque, its upscale neighbor Hannam-dong, the hillside bar streets nearby, what to shop for, an important piece of the neighborhood's recent history worth knowing about, and the practical details (subway stations, timing) for a first visit.

## What Is Itaewon?

Itaewon's international character goes back decades, shaped in part by its proximity to a nearby U.S. military base and by successive waves of foreign residents, diplomats, and businesspeople settling in the area. Over time it grew into Seoul's most cosmopolitan neighborhood, a place where you're as likely to hear English, Arabic, or French on the street as Korean.

That history shows up most clearly in the food. Itaewon's main streets are lined with restaurants representing cuisines from around the world, Middle Eastern, Indian, African, Central Asian, European, and more, in a density you won't really find anywhere else in Seoul. If you've been in Korea for a while and are craving something distinctly non-Korean, this is generally the neighborhood people head to.

## A Muslim-Friendly Neighborhood

A short uphill walk from Itaewon Station (generally cited as being a few minutes up from Exit 1 or Exit 3, worth confirming your exact route with a map app) sits the **Seoul Central Mosque**, Korea's first mosque, built in 1976. The streets around it have grown into a genuine Muslim-friendly district, with halal restaurants, halal butchers and grocers, Islamic bookstores, and travel agencies clustered along the hill.

This makes the area a reliable option for Muslim travelers looking for halal food in Seoul, and a worthwhile stop for anyone curious about this side of the city's international character, regardless of your own background.

## Hannam-dong: Seoul's Upscale, Gallery-Filled Neighbor

Just next to Itaewon sits **Hannam-dong**, a noticeably more polished neighborhood known for art galleries, concept stores, and cafes with real design ambition. The stretch of road between Itaewon and Hangangjin stations, sometimes nicknamed "Comme des Garçons Street," has developed a reputation as a gallery-and-boutique strip in its own right.

The centerpiece is the **Leeum Museum of Art**, a major private art museum with both traditional Korean art and contemporary international works. Regular hours generally run **10 AM to 6 PM**, closed on Mondays as well as Lunar New Year and Chuseok holidays, and some special exhibitions require advance ticket reservations, worth checking the museum's official site before you go if there's a specific show you want to see.

If Hannam-dong's mix of galleries and quiet, design-forward cafes appeals to you, it's a similar spirit to [Seongsu's converted-factory art and cafe scene](https://korea-travel-guide-kr.blogspot.com/2026/07/seongsu-travel-guide.html), just with a noticeably more upscale, polished feel here compared to Seongsu's industrial edge.

## Gyeongnidan-gil: The Hillside Bar and Cafe Streets

West of the main Itaewon strip, a network of sloped alleys known as **Gyeongnidan-gil** (officially Hoenamu-gil) climbs a hillside packed with small bars, cafes, and restaurants. It has a distinctly different feel from the main Itaewon drag, quieter, more residential, and popular for cafe-hopping by day and a more low-key drinking scene by night.

It's reached from **Noksapyeong Station**, a short uphill walk from the station rather than right at the exit, so pace yourself if you're not used to hills.

## Shopping: Antiques, Vintage, and Custom Shoes

Itaewon has a shopping identity that's distinct from Seoul's other districts. It's long been known for antique and vintage dealers, a holdover from decades of foreign residents buying and selling furniture and collectibles, along with a cluster of custom and made-to-order shoe shops that developed a local reputation over the years. It's a different kind of shopping than the fast-fashion pace of Myeongdong or Dongdaemun, slower, more specialized, and worth a browse if either of those categories interests you.

## An Important Part of Itaewon's Recent History

Before we cover timing and getting there, it's worth pausing on something. On the night of **October 29, 2022**, a crowd crush during Halloween festivities in a narrow alley near Itaewon Station killed **159 people** and injured many more, one of the deadliest disasters in the country's modern history. It remains a painful, present part of the neighborhood's story, memorials and quiet remembrance are still part of Itaewon, particularly around that date each year.

We're not including this to be sensational, and we won't describe the event beyond these basic facts. We're including it because it's relevant, respectfully, to visiting: it's a reminder to be mindful of crowd density during major events like Halloween, to follow any official crowd-safety guidance in place, and to treat the neighborhood, and its history, with a bit of care.

## Best Time to Visit

Itaewon's restaurant and nightlife streets are liveliest in the evening, particularly on weekends, while Hannam-dong's galleries and cafes are better suited to a daytime visit. If you're heading to the mosque area or shopping for antiques and vintage goods, daytime hours work best since that's when most shops are actually open.

## How to Get There by Subway

**Itaewon Station (Line 6)** is the main stop for Itaewon itself, with four exits. **Exit 1** or **Exit 4** lead most directly into the main international restaurant strip, and the halal food area around the mosque is a short uphill walk from around Exit 1 or Exit 3, it's worth double-checking your exact route with Naver Map or KakaoMap once you're there rather than guessing.

For **Hannam-dong** and the gallery strip toward Leeum, **Hangangjin Station (Line 6)** is the more direct option, one stop from Itaewon Station. For **Gyeongnidan-gil**, use **Noksapyeong Station (Line 6)**, one stop in the other direction, then walk uphill from Exit 2.

Our [Seoul subway guide](https://korea-travel-guide-kr.blogspot.com/2026/07/seoul-subway-guide.html) covers how T-money, transfers, and station navigation work in general if any of this is new to you.

## Quick Recap

Itaewon is Seoul's most international neighborhood, built on decades of foreign residents and visitors, and it shows in the food, the Muslim-friendly streets around the central mosque, and a shopping scene built around antiques and vintage rather than fast fashion. Next door, Hannam-dong offers a more polished gallery-and-cafe experience anchored by the Leeum Museum, and Gyeongnidan-gil adds a quieter hillside bar-and-cafe scene to the west. It's also a neighborhood with a genuinely painful recent history worth being aware of and respectful toward, particularly if your visit falls around Halloween.
