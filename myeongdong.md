# Myeongdong: Your First Stop for K-Beauty, Street Food, and Sightseeing in Seoul

If you only have time for one shopping district in Seoul, most first-time visitors end up here. Myeongdong is easy to reach, easy to walk around, and packed with things that are hard to find all in one place anywhere else in the city: cosmetics shops on every corner, street food carts on every block, and a few real landmarks mixed in between.

This guide covers what Myeongdong actually is, what to shop for, what to eat, where to go nearby, and the practical details (subway stations, tax refunds, timing) that make a first visit go smoothly.

## What Is Myeongdong?

Myeongdong is a pedestrian shopping district in central Seoul, in the Jung-gu area near City Hall. It has been one of Seoul's main shopping areas for decades, and today it's especially known for K-beauty stores, affordable fashion, and street food.

During the day and into the evening, the main streets are closed to most car traffic, so it's easy to just walk from shop to shop. Store staff in this area are used to foreign tourists, and many shops have English-speaking staff or English signage, which makes it a comfortable place to start if you're still getting used to being in Korea.

## K-Beauty Shopping: Road Shops and Sampling

Myeongdong is one of the best places in Seoul to try Korean skincare and makeup without committing to buying it first. Korean beauty brands often run "road shops," street-level stores where you can walk in, test products on your hand, and ask staff for recommendations, no pressure to buy.

You'll see well-known Korean beauty brands with their own storefronts, plus multi-brand stores that carry dozens of different Korean skincare and makeup lines under one roof. These multi-brand stores are especially useful if you don't know exactly what you want yet, since staff can help you compare a few options side by side.

A few tips: prices in these stores are usually fixed and clearly marked, so there's no need to negotiate. Many stores also offer duty-free pricing or an extra discount if you show your passport, so keep it with you while shopping.

## Street Food You Have to Try

Myeongdong's street food stalls usually set up in the late afternoon and run into the evening, lining the main pedestrian streets with steam, smoke, and long lines.

Common things to look for:

- **Tteokbokki** – chewy rice cakes in a spicy-sweet red sauce, a Korean street food staple.
- **Hotteok** – a sweet, crispy-fried pancake filled with brown sugar, cinnamon, and nuts.
- **Gyeran-bbang** – "egg bread," a small loaf of bread baked with a whole egg on top.
- **Skewered and grilled foods** – everything from grilled squid to giant shrimp skewers, sold fresh off the grill.
- **Cheese-topped and fusion snacks** – Myeongdong is known for trend-driven street food, so you'll often find newer, more Instagram-friendly items alongside the classics.

Most stalls are cash-friendly and increasingly accept card payment too, but it's still smart to carry some small cash in case a particular stall doesn't take cards.

## Nearby Sights: Myeongdong Cathedral and More

Right at the edge of the shopping streets sits **Myeongdong Cathedral**, one of Seoul's most recognizable landmarks. Built in Gothic style and completed in 1898, it was the first brick cathedral built in Korea and remains an important site in Korean Catholic history, including its role as a gathering place during pro-democracy protests in the 1970s and 80s.

The cathedral grounds and interior are open to visitors every day, generally from around 9 AM to 8 PM, with Sunday hours tending to start a bit earlier. Admission is free. Interior viewing can be limited while Mass is being held, so if you want a full look inside, it helps to time your visit around the Mass schedule. The building on the grounds that's closed on Mondays isn't the cathedral itself, it's the separate Archdiocese of Seoul history museum next door. Because opening hours for tourists can change, it's worth checking the cathedral's official information before you plan your visit around it.

Beyond the cathedral, Myeongdong sits close to several large department stores (including branches of Lotte and Shinsegae) and is within easy walking or short-taxi distance of City Hall and Seoul Plaza, making it a convenient hub to combine with other central Seoul sightseeing.

## Shopping Tip: How to Get Your Tax Refund

As a foreign tourist, you can usually get part of Korea's value-added tax (VAT) back on purchases made at participating stores, and Myeongdong has several downtown tax refund kiosks specifically because it's such a popular shopping area.

Here's roughly how it works:

1. **Shop at stores with a "Tax Free" sign.** Not every store participates, so look for the sign or ask before you buy.
2. **Meet the minimum purchase amount.** This varies by store, generally somewhere in the 15,000–30,000 KRW range per transaction, so check the store's policy if you're close to the line.
3. **Bring your passport when you shop.** You'll usually need it at the time of purchase to get a tax-free form or receipt.
4. **Claim your refund.** You can often get an immediate refund at a downtown kiosk (Myeongdong has these), or claim it later at the airport when you leave the country.

The refund is based on Korea's 10% VAT, but after processing fees, the actual cash you get back is usually smaller than that, commonly cited as somewhere in the mid-single-digit percentage range. Rules and minimums do change, so it's worth checking the current details on the Visit Korea or Visit Seoul official tourism sites before you rely on exact numbers.

## How to Get to Myeongdong by Subway

Myeongdong sits between two subway stations, so which one you use depends on where in Myeongdong you're headed.

**Myeongdong Station (Line 4)** has several exits that open directly onto the shopping district. Exit 6 puts you right at the heart of the main shopping street, while Exit 10 leads more directly toward Myeongdong Cathedral. The cathedral is also within walking distance of Euljiro 3(sam)-ga Station (Lines 2 and 3), via Exit 12.

**Euljiro 1(il)-ga Station (Line 2)** sits on the opposite side of the district, about a 2-minute walk from the edge of Myeongdong. Exit 6 leads toward Myeongdong-gil (the main street), and Exit 7 comes out near the Lotte Hotel and Lotte Department Store.

The two stations are roughly a 10-minute walk apart, with the shopping district in between, so either one works as a starting point depending on your route that day.

## Best Time to Visit

Myeongdong is busy most days, but it's noticeably more crowded on weekend afternoons and evenings, when both tourists and local shoppers are out at the same time. If you'd rather shop without fighting through crowds, weekday mornings tend to be quieter.

Street food stalls are mainly an afternoon-into-evening thing, so if trying street food is a priority, plan to be there later in the day rather than first thing in the morning. Most shops open around 10 or 11 AM and stay open into the evening, often past 10 PM, but exact hours vary store by store, so don't count on anything being open very early.

## Nearby Combo Course: Euljiro and Gwangjang Market

If you want to turn Myeongdong into a half-day or full-day route instead of a quick stop, it pairs naturally with two nearby neighborhoods.

**Euljiro** is within walking distance and has become known in recent years for its mix of old hardware and printing shops alongside a wave of trendy retro-style cafes and bars tucked into the same buildings. It's a good contrast to Myeongdong's polished shopping streets if you want to see a different side of the city.

**Gwangjang Market**, one of Seoul's oldest and most famous traditional markets, is a bit further out but easy to reach by subway, generally by heading toward Euljiro 4-ga Station or Dongdaemun. It's especially known for **bindaetteok** (savory mung bean pancakes), **mayak gimbap** (small, addictive seaweed rice rolls), tteokbokki, and sundae (Korean blood sausage), all cooked right in front of you by market vendors.

Combining all three in one day makes for a route that moves from modern shopping (Myeongdong) to retro cool (Euljiro) to traditional market food (Gwangjang), all connected by a short subway ride.

## Quick Recap

Myeongdong works well as a first stop in Seoul because it's easy to reach, easy to navigate, and covers shopping, food, and sightseeing all in one walkable area. Come for the K-beauty road shops, stay for the street food, swing by Myeongdong Cathedral while you're there, and don't forget to ask about your tax refund before you leave the store. If you have extra time, Euljiro and Gwangjang Market are both an easy add-on from here.

*Opening hours, prices, and tax refund rules can change. Check the official Visit Seoul (english.visitseoul.net) and Visit Korea (english.visitkorea.or.kr) sites for the latest details before you go.*
