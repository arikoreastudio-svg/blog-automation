# Seoul 4-Day Itinerary for First-Time Visitors

Seoul has enough neighborhoods, palaces, and food streets to fill two weeks, but most first-time visitors only have three or four days to work with. This itinerary is built around that reality: it groups things by geography so you're not crossing the city back and forth, and it links out to a full guide for nearly every stop, so you can dig into the details (exact subway exits, opening hours, prices, safety rules) without this single post turning into an encyclopedia.

Think of this as the table of contents for your trip. Read this for the big picture and the day-by-day flow, then click through to the individual guides for the specifics once you know where you're actually headed.

## Before Day 1: Arrival Day

However you land, our [guide to getting from Incheon Airport to Seoul](https://korea-travel-guide-kr.blogspot.com/2026/07/incheon-airport-to-seoul.html) compares the AREX express train, the AREX all-stop train, the airport limousine bus, and taxis, worth reading before you land so you're not deciding on the spot.

Whichever option you pick, this is also the moment to pick up a **T-money card**, either at a convenience store in the arrivals area or a machine near the AREX platform. It's the transit card you'll use every single day of this itinerary, on the subway and on buses. Our [Seoul subway guide](https://korea-travel-guide-kr.blogspot.com/2026/07/seoul-subway-guide.html) covers how T-money, fares, and transfers actually work, plus one thing worth doing right at the airport: download **Naver Map or KakaoMap** before you need them. Google Maps doesn't give reliable walking or transit directions in Korea, and it's the single most common thing that trips up first-time visitors here.

Once you've checked into your hotel, keep the rest of arrival day light. Jet lag is real, and Days 1 through 3 all involve a fair amount of walking.

## Day 1: Gyeongbokgung, Bukchon, and Hanbok

This day stays almost entirely within walking distance of itself, one palace, one historic village, and a couple of nearby neighborhoods to wind down in.

**Morning: Gyeongbokgung Palace, ideally in hanbok.** Our [Gyeongbokgung and hanbok guide](https://korea-travel-guide-kr.blogspot.com/2026/07/gyeongbokgung-hanbok-guide.html) covers the full details, but the short version: if you rent a proper hanbok (a full top-and-bottom set) from one of the shops near the station, you get into the palace for free. Try to time your visit around the Royal Guard Changing Ceremony at 10:00 or 14:00. One thing to double-check before you build your whole morning around this: **the palace is closed on Tuesdays.**

**Afternoon: Walk to Bukchon Hanok Village.** Bukchon is a short walk from Gyeongbokgung and looks its best with the light of hanbok photos still fresh. Our [Bukchon guide](https://korea-travel-guide-kr.blogspot.com/2026/07/bukchon-hanok-village-guide.html) has one rule worth knowing before you arrive: the busiest lanes are only open for sightseeing between **10:00 AM and 5:00 PM**, with a real fine for wandering around taking photos outside that window. It's also a residential neighborhood, so keep your voice down and don't peek into open gates, small courtesies that go a long way there.

**Evening: Samcheong-dong or Insadong.** Both sit right next to Bukchon. Samcheong-dong is the easier wind-down, a cafe-lined street for coffee or dessert after a day of walking. Insadong has more energy if you want tea houses, galleries, and traditional crafts to browse before dinner.

## Day 2: Myeongdong, Euljiro, and Gwangjang Market

This route moves from modern shopping to a retro pocket of the city to one of Seoul's oldest food markets, all connected by a short subway ride or an easy walk.

**Morning: Myeongdong.** Our [Myeongdong guide](https://korea-travel-guide-kr.blogspot.com/2026/07/myeongdong-travel-guide.html) covers K-beauty road shops, street food, and Myeongdong Cathedral. If you're shopping, keep your passport on you, many stores offer duty-free pricing or a tax refund, and Myeongdong has downtown kiosks specifically for this.

**Afternoon: Euljiro, then Gwangjang Market.** Euljiro is a short walk from Myeongdong and worth a quick detour for its mix of old hardware shops and trendy retro cafes tucked into the same buildings. From there, head to **Gwangjang Market**, one of Korea's oldest markets, open since 1905. Our [Gwangjang Market guide](https://korea-travel-guide-kr.blogspot.com/2026/07/gwangjang-market-guide.html) walks through the must-try foods (bindaetteok and mayak gimbap are the two most iconic), but the practical tip worth remembering here: **only order from stalls with a visible price list**, and confirm the total before you sit down. It's a real, documented issue at this specific market, not just general travel caution.

**Evening: Dinner at the market.** The food alley at Gwangjang Market stays open later than the general retail section, into the evening, so it works fine as a dinner stop rather than just a lunch detour.

## Day 3: Seongsu and Seoul Forest

Seongsu is far enough from the other clusters that it deserves its own day rather than being squeezed in as an add-on.

**Morning and afternoon: Seongsu-dong.** Our [Seongsu guide](https://korea-travel-guide-kr.blogspot.com/2026/07/seongsu-travel-guide.html) covers what makes the neighborhood distinct: cafes built inside old shoe factories and warehouses, rotating fashion and K-pop pop-up stores, and independent concept shops that feel more like small galleries than regular retail. Since pop-ups change every few weeks, it's worth checking what's currently running before you go rather than chasing a specific store from an older list.

**Evening: Seoul Forest.** Right next to Seongsu, this is one of Seoul's bigger city parks, open lawns and walking paths, a nice contrast after a day spent around brick and concrete. It's an easy, low-effort way to end your last full day in the city.

## Adjusting the Plan

**If you only have 2–3 days instead of 4:** Day 1 (Gyeongbokgung and Bukchon) and Day 2 (Myeongdong and Gwangjang Market) are the two clusters most worth keeping intact, since each pairs a must-see with a short walking add-on. Seongsu is the easiest day to cut if you're short on time, it's the one cluster that isn't essential to a first Seoul trip, more of a "if you have the extra day" addition.

**If you have more than 4 days:** Use the extra time to slow down rather than add a fifth cluster from scratch, Bukchon and Seongsu especially reward unhurried wandering rather than a checklist pace. Extra days are also a good time to revisit Gwangjang Market or Myeongdong for a second, more relaxed round of eating and shopping.

**If it's raining:** Myeongdong (mostly covered arcades and indoor stores) and Gwangjang Market (an indoor market) hold up fine in bad weather. Bukchon and Seoul Forest are both outdoors and lose a lot of their appeal in the rain, so swap those for an indoor day if the forecast looks bad, and save the outdoor days for clearer weather later in your trip.

## Quick Recap

Day 1 covers Gyeongbokgung, Bukchon, and Samcheong-dong or Insadong, all walkable from each other. Day 2 covers Myeongdong, Euljiro, and Gwangjang Market, connected by a short subway ride. Day 3 is Seongsu and Seoul Forest on their own. Get a T-money card and Naver Map or KakaoMap set up on arrival day, and click through to each linked guide for the exact details, exits, hours, and rules, once you know which day you're planning.

*Opening hours, entry rules, and prices at each stop can change. Check the individual guides linked above, and the official Visit Seoul site (english.visitseoul.net), for the latest details before you finalize your plan.*
